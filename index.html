01 — Research interests

What I'm working on

Four connected interests, one question: what happens to people when software and AI organize their work — and what should we build once we know? I study it with mixed methods, then write the tools the findings call for.

01 · Interest

AI and the future of work

How automation, generative AI and algorithmic management reshape jobs, skills, authority and inequality — starting with the workers who feel it first. Published in Sociology Lens, with a second study at revise-and-resubmit.

Why it mattersEmployers are adopting AI faster than anyone is measuring what it does to the people working alongside it. Evidence has to arrive before the policy does.
02 · Interest

Human–computer interaction

How people actually work with AI agents, interfaces and online tools. At Syracuse I design interactive web tools and AI-supported learning resources for Gravity Spy, the citizen-science project that helps LIGO classify detector noise.

Why it mattersGood design decides whether AI helps people or quietly pushes them out. HCI turns that question into something we can test and build.
03 · Interest

Digital labor

Platform work, gig work and the ratings, deadlines and algorithms that manage it. I study how digital labor markets move work across borders — and who carries the risk when they do.

Why it mattersA growing share of the work behind US and European firms happens on platforms, beyond the reach of most employment law.
04 · Interest

Online freelancers

My master's thesis follows Bangladeshi freelancers selling into global markets on Upwork and Fiverr — 24 in-depth interviews paired with secondary quantitative data. I was one of them first: 100+ client projects before the PhD.

Why it mattersMillions of people in the Global South now earn a living online. How they adapt to AI will shape the future of work far beyond any one country.
